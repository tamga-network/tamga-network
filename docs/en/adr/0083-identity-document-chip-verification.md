# In short

Identity cards and passports contain a chip. The chip holds the person's name, date of birth and photo, together with the
state's signature. A phone can read this chip over NFC. With this decision, Tamga takes the attributes of the identity
credential from the chip instead of from a photo of the document, and checks the state's signature itself. To show that the
person really holds that document, face and liveness checks are still done by an external provider: the live face is compared
with the photo on the chip. The correctness of the attributes then rests on the state's signature, not on the provider's
software; the provider is needed only for the face part and can be replaced. For documents without a chip, today's photo path
remains.

# Context

- **Today.** The identity service ([[ADR-0011]]) sends the person to a remote identity verification provider, which reads the
  document photo, checks liveness and the face, and says "approved". The attributes are the values the provider read; the
  identity service cannot verify them. Class: non-qualified EAA, I2 ([[ADR-0022]]).
- **Provider dependency.** The provider's free allowance has ended; every verification is paid. The cost is small, but the whole
  assurance of identity verification depends on one provider's software and pricing.
- **How PID is issued in the EU.** A PID provider under a member state's eID scheme issues PID; wallet users are enrolled under
  the enrolment rules of assurance level high (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2977 Art. 3). For remote enrolment,
  Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/798 relies on ETSI TS 119 461 V2.1.1 and requires the face image of a physical document to be
  **collected from the chip over NFC**. In ETSI TS 119 461 the fully automated "document + face" path requires a chip document;
  the extended level also requires lab-tested presentation attack detection and injection attack detection (CEN/TS 18099).
- **Tamga's position.** Tamga does not and will not issue PID (TL8). The identity credential is a provisional EAA that gives way
  to state PID ([[ADR-0011]] K1), but it is the basis of identity matching for every institutional credential on the network.
- **Foreseen earlier.** [[ADR-0011]] anticipated chip reading; `document_chip_verified` and
  `verification_method: remote-nfc-liveness-face` exist in the schema. The open question is who reads and who verifies the chip.
- **A browser cannot read the chip.** A native app is required: either the provider's mobile component embedded in the wallet,
  or the wallet itself.
- **Link relay attack ([[ADR-0080]]).** If the chip is read on the phone that started the flow, an attacker must physically tap
  the victim's card on their own phone; a relayed link alone no longer works.

# Decision

- **K1 — Chip path.** The identity service offers (a) the **chip path** — the document chip is read over NFC in the wallet that
  started the flow, face and liveness are checked by a provider; and (b) the **document photo path** — today's path, as a
  fallback for documents without a chip and phones without NFC.
- **K2 — The wallet is only a reader.** The wallet reads the ICAO 9303 data groups (at least DG1, DG2 and the document security
  object SOD; DG14 and DG15 if present) and relays them **unchanged**. It does not interpret, decide on, store or log chip data.
  The chip access key (MRZ or CAN) is used only on the phone and is not sent to the identity service.
- **K3 — The identity service verifies.** Server-side: (1) **passive authentication** — the SOD signature against the document
  signer certificate and the issuing state's CSCA, and data group hashes against the SOD; (2) **clone check** — active
  authentication (AA) or chip authentication (CA) where supported; for AA the challenge is generated by the identity service, is
  single-use and bound to the flow. A verification result reported by the phone is not accepted.
- **K4 — Root certificates.** CSCA certificates are taken from public, signed master lists; which states are covered and where
  they come from is recorded in the operator's configuration document. A document without a known root falls back to the photo path.
- **K5 — Narrower meaning of `document_chip_verified`.** `true` only when passive authentication, the clone check and the match
  of the live face with DG2 all succeed. If passive authentication succeeds but the chip supports no clone check, the credential
  may still be issued from chip data with `document_chip_verified: false` and `verification_method: remote-document-liveness-face`.
- **K6 — Face and liveness stay with a provider** that has lab-tested presentation attack detection (ISO/IEC 30107-3) and
  injection attack testing. Liveness runs in the provider's browser flow (the wallet does not talk to the provider); the live face
  is compared with DG2. The provider sits behind an adapter.
- **K7 — Retention.** Chip data, the DG2 photo and the face image are kept in memory only until issuance (IDP4, IDP9 unchanged);
  only the keyed hash of the document number, the fact that the chip path was used and pass/fail results are recorded.
- **K8 — Opening per country.** The chip path opens on the live network for a country only when (a) its root certificate comes
  from a trusted source and (b) the legal review of a private body reading the identity chip with consent and processing
  biometric data under that country's law is complete. It may be tried in the sandbox with test documents.
- **K9 — IDP3 narrowed.** New text: "IDV provider integration is only in the Tamga identity service; institutional issuers,
  wallets and verifiers do not talk to the provider. A wallet may read the document chip on the device that started the flow and
  relay the raw data unchanged to the identity service; it does not verify chip data and makes no identity verification decision."
- **K10 — Open interface.** Relaying chip data is defined as an open interface in [[SPEC-ID-0003]]; any listed conformant wallet
  may implement it ([[ADR-0042]]).

# Options considered

| Option | Outcome | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Wallet reads the chip, identity service verifies, provider does face/liveness** | **proposed** | Data verified by the state's signature; less provider dependency; relay attack harder |
| Today's path only | kept as fallback | Works and is cheap, but assurance depends on the provider and does not reach the EU high level |
| Provider's own chip reading (its mobile component in the wallet) | rejected | Ties the wallet to one provider, against IDP3 and [[ADR-0042]] |
| Building face and liveness ourselves | rejected | Presentation and injection attacks need expertise and independent testing |
| Separate identity verification app | not now | A second app for users, operated by the network |
| Authoritative source only (state, bank, operator) | long-term direction | No accessible path today; added alongside, not instead |

# Status

**Proposed — 2026-11-13.** Not accepted; no code changes with this decision.
